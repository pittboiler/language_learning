### Am / is / are: сум

**Agenda:** Say am, is, are: сум, си, е and friends

**Rule card:** сум means “am”, and it changes with the person: јас сум, ти си, тој/таа е, ние сме, вие сте, тие се. It never starts a sentence; something always comes first: Добро сум (“I'm well”), Јас сум Ана.

**Recap:** The verb for am/is/are changes with who you mean: сум (I), си (you), е (he/she/it), сме (we), сте (you all, or polite you), се (they). It's a light word that leans on the word before it, so it never opens a sentence: Добро сум, фала. Because the form already says who, you can usually drop јас: Добро сум is a complete “I'm well”. You'll hear it all through this chapter: Ана е во Скопје (“Ana is in Skopje”), Од Германија сум (“I'm from Germany”).

**Examples:**
- Ана е во Скопје. — *Ana is in Skopje.* `story:gen-s0-repair-story#0`
- Да, точно. Од Германија сум. — *Yes, exactly. I'm from Germany.* `scenario:gen-s0-repair#7`
- Јас сум Ана — *I am Ana* `grammar:to-be#0`
- Ние сме тука — *We are here* `grammar:to-be#2`

<details><summary>Library page</summary>

The present tense of “to be” has six forms: сум (I am), си (you are), е (he/she/it is), сме (we are), сте (you all are, or polite you are), се (they are). These forms are unstressed: they lean on the word before them, so they can't start a sentence. Put a subject or another word first: Јас сум Ана, Тоа е кафе, Добро сум. Since the form already shows the person, the pronoun is usually left out unless you want to stress it. To make it negative, put не in front: не е, не сум.

**Why it's like this**
- Why can't сум start a sentence? It's unstressed, so it needs a word in front to lean on. That's why it's Добро сум (“I'm well”), with сум second.
- Why is јас often missing? сум can only mean “I am”, so јас is added only for emphasis or contrast: И јас сум добро (“I'm fine too”).
- Watch the look-alike: се means “they are” here, but the same little word also appears in Се гледаме (“See you”). Chapter 5 explains that second job.
- сте is both “you all are” and the polite “you are” to one person, so it pairs with вие either way (chapter 2 covers ти and вие).

**Common mistakes**
- Not opening with сум, but leaning it on a word first: Добро сум, Јас сум Ана.
- Not using сум for everyone: “you are” is си (or сте when polite), “she is” is е.
- Not adding a pronoun every time: Добро сум already means “I'm well”.

</details>

**Grammar cards**
- 🃏 *How do you say “I am”, “you are”, “he/she/it is”?* → сум, си, е: Јас сум Ана, Како си?, Тоа е кафе. (e.g. Јас сум Ана)
- 🃏 *Can сум start a sentence?* → No. Something comes first: Добро сум. (e.g. „Добро сум.“)

- ▢ Како ___ Марко? → **е** of [е / си / сум]: Marko is “he” → е.

### Saying “not”: не

**Agenda:** Say “not”: put не right before the verb

**Rule card:** To make a sentence negative, put не directly in front of the verb: Не разбирам (“I don't understand”), Не знам (“I don't know”). Nothing goes between не and the verb.

**Recap:** Today's rule is one small word: не, right before the verb. You used it in the two phrases a beginner needs most, Не разбирам and Не знам. The verb stays exactly as it was; не just sits in front of it. It works the same way with “is”: Тоа не е точно means “That isn't right”. There's no extra word like English “do”: “I don't know” is simply Не знам.

**Examples:**
- „Извинете, не разбирам.“ — *"Excuse me, I don't understand."* `story:gen-s0-repair-story#2`
- Не разбирам. — *I don't understand.* `qa:gen-s0-repair-story#q1:a`
- Тоа не е точно — *That isn't right* `grammar:negation#1`
- Не сакам чај — *I don't want tea* `grammar:negation#0`

<details><summary>Library page</summary>

Macedonian makes almost any sentence negative with one small word, не, placed immediately before the verb: разбирам → не разбирам, знам → не знам, сакам → не сакам. With am/is/are it goes in front of that word too: Тоа не е точно (“That isn't right”). не and its verb are said together, in one breath, with no pause between them. On its own at the start of a reply, Не simply means “No”: Не, фала (“No, thanks”).

**Why it's like this**
- Why right before the verb? Macedonian treats не and the verb as a pair said in one breath, so не always sits directly in front of it: Не разбирам.
- Two jobs for one word: on its own at the start of a reply, Не means “No”; in front of a verb it means “not”. You can hear both in Не, сѐ уште учи (“No, she's still learning”).
- There's no helper word like English “do”: “I don't know” is just Не знам, literally “not I-know”.
- For “there isn't” or “don't have”, Macedonian uses a different word, нема (Нема проблем, “No problem”). That one comes in chapter 12; for now, treat Нема проблем as a set phrase.

**Common mistakes**
- Not putting не after the verb, but before it: Не разбирам.
- Not adding an extra word for “do”: “I don't understand” is just Не разбирам.
- Not reading every Не as “not”: at the start of an answer, Не, фала means “No, thanks”.

</details>

**Grammar cards**
- 🃏 *Where does не go to make a sentence negative?* → Right before the verb: Не разбирам, Не знам. (e.g. Не разбирам.)
- 🃏 *How do you say “I don't know”? Is there a word for “do”?* → No “do” word: just Не знам.
- ▢ „Извинете, ___ разбирам.“ → **не** of [не / да / ли]: не right before the verb makes it “don't understand”.
- ▢ Тоа ___ е точно → **не** of [не / ли / сум]: не goes right in front of е: “isn't”.

### Question words

**Agenda:** Ask what, where, how, how much: question words go first

**Rule card:** Start the question with the question word: Што значи …? (“What does … mean?”), Како се вели …? (“How do you say…?”), Колку чини? (“How much is it?”). With a question word you don't need ли or дали.

**Recap:** You've been asking Што значи …? and Како се вели …? since chapter 1; now here's how they work. Today's question words are што (what), каде (where), како (how) and колку (how much / how many). Each one goes at the very start, and the rest follows in normal order: Што значи 'фала'? A question word already makes it a question, so there's no ли or дали. If there's a little word like од (“from”), it comes along to the front: Од каде сте? (“Where are you from?”). Two more you'll meet soon: кој (who) and зошто (why).

Questions that ask for information rather than yes or no begin with a question word: што (what), кој (who), каде (where), кога (when), зошто (why), како (how), колку (how much / how many). - Why no ли here? ли and дали are only for yes/no questions. A question word already makes the sentence a question.

**Common mistakes**
- Not leaving the question word at the end, as in English “You're from where?”: it goes first, Од каде сте?

Like many European languages, Macedonian splits “you” in two. ти is singular and familiar: friends, family, children, people your own age in relaxed settings. вие is for one person you address politely (a waiter, a shopkeeper, an older person, anyone you've just met in a formal setting) and also for any group. Everything that refers to “you” follows the choice: си or сте for “are”, and verb endings in -ш or -те (Сакаш or Сакате). The polite words you already know, Извинете and Повелете, are вие forms too.

**Why it's like this**
- Why does Можете ли да повторите? end in -те? It's the polite вие form, the natural choice when you ask a stranger to repeat.
- вие does double duty: polite “you” to one person, and “you all” to a group. Context tells you which.
- The greeting often signals the register: Здраво goes with ти, Добар ден with вие.
- Moving from вие to ти is a step toward friendliness. With older people, let them offer it.

**Common mistakes**
- Not mixing the two in one sentence: Како сте? (polite) or Како си? (casual), with the verb matching the “you” you picked.
- Not using ти with a waiter or shopkeeper you don't know: вие is the safe default.

### Yes/no questions: ли or дали

**Agenda:** Ask yes/no questions: ли after the verb, or дали up front

**Rule card:** To ask a yes/no question, put ли right after the verb: Можете ли да повторите? (“Can you repeat?”). Or start the question with дали instead: Дали сакаш кафе? Both work the same way.

**Recap:** You now have two ways to turn a statement into a yes/no question. ли sits right after the verb, so Можете (“you can”) becomes Можете ли…? (“can you…?”). дали does the same job from the front: Дали Ана знае добро македонски? You've been saying Можете ли да повторите? since chapter 1: that's ли at work. In it, the да is a separate little word that links “can” to “repeat”. It has nothing to do with the question, and chapter 4 explains it. You'll also hear questions asked with just a rising voice, like Сакаш кафе?

<details><summary>Library page</summary>

Macedonian marks a yes/no question with a small word instead of flipping the word order the way English does. In everyday set phrases the usual choice is ли, placed immediately after the verb: Имате ли …? (“Do you have…?”), Може ли …? (“Can I…? / Is it possible?”), Можете ли да повторите? (“Can you repeat?”). The other option is дали at the very start, with the rest of the sentence left as a normal statement: Дали сакаш кафе? (“Do you want coffee?”). Both are correct and mean the same thing. Short requests tend to use ли; дали is handy for longer questions. In relaxed speech, a plain statement with a rising voice also works.

**Why it's like this**
- Why does ли come after the verb? ли can't stand on its own, so it leans on the word being asked about, almost always the verb: Имате ли…? is literally “have-you (question)…?”.
- Why “ли да” in Можете ли да повторите? They're two separate words doing two jobs. ли makes it a question. да links two verbs, because Macedonian has no “to repeat” form: “you can … that you repeat”. Chapter 4 covers да.
- With am/is/are, use дали. Forms like си and е can't carry ли, so “Are you here?” is Дали си тука?
- Don't confuse the two да words. Да on its own means “yes”; the да inside a sentence is the linking word.

**Common mistakes**
- Not starting a sentence with ли: it always follows the verb, as in Можете ли…?
- Not using дали and ли together: pick one, either Дали сакаш кафе? or Сакаш ли кафе?
- Not adding a word for “do”: “Do you have water?” is just Имате ли вода?

</details>
