### Saying “not”: не

**Agenda:** Say “not”: put не right before the verb

**Rule card:** To make a sentence negative, put не directly in front of the verb: Не разбирам (“I don't understand”), Не знам (“I don't know”). Nothing goes between не and the verb.

**Recap:** Today's rule is one small word: не, right before the verb. You used it in the two phrases a beginner needs most, Не разбирам and Не знам. The verb stays exactly as it was; не just sits in front of it. It works the same way with “is”: Тоа не е точно means “That isn't right”. There's no extra word like English “do”: “I don't know” is simply Не знам.

**Examples:**
- „Извинете, не разбирам.“ — *"Excuse me, I don't understand."* `story:gen-s0-repair-story#2`
- Не разбирам. — *I don't understand.* `qa:gen-s0-repair-story#q1:a`
- Тоа не е точно — *That isn't right* `grammar:negation#1`
- Не сакам чај — *I don't want tea* `grammar:negation#0`

<details><summary>Library page</summary>

Macedonian makes almost any sentence negative with one small word, не, placed immediately before the verb: разбирам → не разбирам, знам → не знам, сакам → не сакам. With am/is/are it goes in front of that word too: Тоа не е точно (“That isn't right”). не and its verb are said together, in one breath, with no pause between them. On its own at the start of a reply, Не simply means “No”: Не, фала (“No, thanks”).

**Why it's like this**
- Why right before the verb? Macedonian treats не and the verb as a pair said in one breath, so не always sits directly in front of it: Не разбирам.
- Two jobs for one word: on its own at the start of a reply, Не means “No”; in front of a verb it means “not”. You can hear both in Не, сѐ уште учи (“No, she's still learning”).
- There's no helper word like English “do”: “I don't know” is just Не знам, literally “not I-know”.
- For “there isn't” or “don't have”, Macedonian uses a different word, нема (Нема проблем, “No problem”). That one comes in chapter 12; for now, treat Нема проблем as a set phrase.

**Common mistakes**
- Not putting не after the verb, but before it: Не разбирам.
- Not adding an extra word for “do”: “I don't understand” is just Не разбирам.
- Not reading every Не as “not”: at the start of an answer, Не, фала means “No, thanks”.

</details>

**Grammar cards**
- 🃏 *Where does не go to make a sentence negative?* → Right before the verb: Не разбирам, Не знам. (e.g. Не разбирам.)
- 🃏 *How do you say “I don't know”? Is there a word for “do”?* → No “do” word: just Не знам.
- ▢ „Изви___те, не разбирам.“ → **не** of [не / да / ли]: не right before the verb makes it “don't understand”.
- ▢ Тоа ___ е точно → **не** of [не / ли / сум]: не goes right in front of е: “isn't”.

### Yes/no questions: ли or дали

**Agenda:** Ask yes/no questions: ли after the verb, or дали up front

**Rule card:** To ask a yes/no question, put ли right after the verb: Можете ли да повторите? (“Can you repeat?”). Or start the question with дали instead: Дали сакаш кафе? Both work the same way.

**Recap:** You now have two ways to turn a statement into a yes/no question. ли sits right after the verb, so Можете (“you can”) becomes Можете ли…? (“can you…?”). дали does the same job from the front: Дали Ана знае добро македонски? In Можете ли да повторите?, the да is a separate little word that links “can” to “repeat”. It has nothing to do with the question, and chapter 4 explains it. You'll also hear questions asked with just a rising voice, like Сакаш кафе?

**Examples:**
- „Можете ли да повторите?“ — *"Can you repeat?"* `story:gen-s0-repair-story#5`
- Дали Ана знае добро македонски? — *Does Ana know Macedonian well?* `qa:gen-s0-repair-story#q3:q`
- Дали сакаш кафе? — *Do you want coffee?* `grammar:questions#0`

<details><summary>Library page</summary>

Macedonian marks a yes/no question with a small word instead of flipping the word order the way English does. In everyday set phrases the usual choice is ли, placed immediately after the verb: Имате ли …? (“Do you have…?”), Може ли …? (“Can I…? / Is it possible?”), Можете ли да повторите? (“Can you repeat?”). The other option is дали at the very start, with the rest of the sentence left as a normal statement: Дали сакаш кафе? (“Do you want coffee?”). Both are correct and mean the same thing. Short requests tend to use ли; дали is handy for longer questions. In relaxed speech, a plain statement with a rising voice also works.

**Why it's like this**
- Why does ли come after the verb? ли can't stand on its own, so it leans on the word being asked about, almost always the verb: Имате ли…? is literally “have-you (question)…?”.
- Why “ли да” in Можете ли да повторите? They're two separate words doing two jobs. ли makes it a question. да links two verbs, because Macedonian has no “to repeat” form: “you can … that you repeat”. Chapter 4 covers да.
- With am/is/are, use дали. Forms like си and е can't carry ли, so “Are you here?” is Дали си тука?
- Don't confuse the two да words. Да on its own means “yes”; the да inside a sentence is the linking word.

**Common mistakes**
- Not starting a sentence with ли: it always follows the verb, as in Можете ли…?
- Not using дали and ли together: pick one, either Дали сакаш кафе? or Сакаш ли кафе?
- Not adding a word for “do”: “Do you have water?” is just Имате ли вода?

</details>

**Grammar cards**
- 🃏 *Where does ли go in a yes/no question?* → Right after the verb: Можете ли…?, Имате ли…? (e.g. „Можете ли да повторите?“)
- 🃏 *What does дали do?* → It starts a yes/no question; the rest stays like a normal sentence: Дали сакаш кафе? (e.g. Дали сакаш кафе?)
- ▢ „Можете ___ да повторите?“ → **ли** of [ли / да / не]: ли right after the verb makes it a yes/no question.
- ▢ ___ Ана знае добро македонски? → **Дали** of [Дали / Што / Каде]: Дали opens a yes/no question.

### Question words

**Agenda:** Ask what, where, how, how much: question words go first

**Rule card:** Start the question with the question word: Што значи …? (“What does … mean?”), Како се вели …? (“How do you say…?”), Колку чини? (“How much is it?”). With a question word you don't need ли or дали.

**Recap:** Today's question words are што (what), каде (where), како (how) and колку (how much / how many). Each one goes at the very start, and the rest follows in normal order: Што значи 'фала'? A question word already makes it a question, so there's no ли or дали. If there's a little word like од (“from”), it comes along to the front: Од каде сте? (“Where are you from?”). Two more you'll meet soon: кој (who) and зошто (why).

**Examples:**
- „Аха. Што значи 'фала'?“ — *"Uh-huh. What does 'fala' mean?"* `story:gen-s0-repair-story#6`
- Аха, разбирам! Како се вели „Germany“ на македонски? — *Ah, I understand! How do you say 'Germany' in Macedonian?* `scenario:gen-s0-repair#5`
- Нема проблем. Од каде сте? — *No problem. Where are you from?* `scenario:gen-s0-repair#2`
- Каде е тоалетот? — *Where's the toilet?* `grammar:questions#2`

<details><summary>Library page</summary>

Questions that ask for information rather than yes or no begin with a question word: што (what), кој (who), каде (where), кога (when), зошто (why), како (how), колку (how much / how many). The question word goes first, and the rest of the sentence follows in normal order: Каде е тоалетот? (“Where's the toilet?”), Што е тоа? (“What is that?”). Because the question word already does the asking, ли and дали aren't used with it.

**Why it's like this**
- Why no ли here? ли and дали are only for yes/no questions. A question word already makes the sentence a question.
- A little word in front travels with it: Од каде сте? is literally “From where are you?”. English leaves “from” at the end; Macedonian keeps it with каде.
- како does a lot of work: Како си? (“How are you?”), Како се вели …? (“How do you say…?”).
- колку covers both “how much” and “how many”: Колку чини? asks a price, Колку јаболка сака Ана? asks a count.

**Common mistakes**
- Not leaving the question word at the end, as in English “You're from where?”: it goes first, Од каде сте?
- Not adding ли to a question that already starts with a question word.
- Not mixing up што and кој: што asks about a thing (Што е тоа?), кој about a person.

</details>

**Grammar cards**
- 🃏 *Where does a question word like што or каде go?* → At the very start: Што значи …?, Каде е …? (e.g. „Аха. Што значи 'фала'?“)
- 🃏 *Do you add ли to a question that starts with што, каде or колку?* → No. The question word already makes it a question. (e.g. Каде е тоалетот?)
- ▢ ___ е тоалетот? → **Каде** of [Каде / Колку / Дали]: каде means “where”.
- ▢ „Аха. ___ значи 'фала'?“ → **Што** of [Што / Каде / Колку]: што means “what”: “What does it mean?”
- ▢ ___ чини? → **Колку** of [Колку / Што / Каде]: колку means “how much”.

### Am / is / are: сум

**Agenda:** Say am, is, are: сум, си, е and friends

**Rule card:** сум means “am”, and it changes with the person: јас сум, ти си, тој/таа е, ние сме, вие сте, тие се. It never starts a sentence; something always comes first: Добро сум (“I'm well”), Јас сум Ана.

**Recap:** The verb for am/is/are changes with who you mean: сум (I), си (you), е (he/she/it), сме (we), сте (you all, or polite you), се (they). It's a light word that leans on the word before it, so it never opens a sentence: Добро сум, фала. Because the form already says who, you can usually drop јас: Добро сум is a complete “I'm well”. You've been hearing it since chapter 1, in Ана е во Скопје and Од Германија сум.

**Examples:**

- Ние сме тука — *We are here* `grammar:to-be#2`
- Како е Марко? — *How is Marko?* `qa:gen-s0-greet-story#q2:q`

<details><summary>Library page</summary>

The present tense of “to be” has six forms: сум (I am), си (you are), е (he/she/it is), сме (we are), сте (you all are, or polite you are), се (they are). These forms are unstressed: they lean on the word before them, so they can't start a sentence. Put a subject or another word first: Јас сум Ана, Тоа е кафе, Добро сум. Since the form already shows the person, the pronoun is usually left out unless you want to stress it. To make it negative, put не in front: не е, не сум.

**Why it's like this**
- Why can't сум start a sentence? It's unstressed, so it needs a word in front to lean on. That's why it's Добро сум (“I'm well”), with сум second.
- Why is јас often missing? сум can only mean “I am”, so јас is added only for emphasis or contrast: И јас сум добро (“I'm fine too”).
- Watch the look-alike: се means “they are” here, but the same little word also appears in Се гледаме (“See you”). Chapter 5 explains that second job.
- сте is both “you all are” and the polite “you are” to one person, so it pairs with вие either way (more in the next lesson).

**Common mistakes**
- Not opening with сум, but leaning it on a word first: Добро сум, Јас сум Ана.
- Not using сум for everyone: “you are” is си (or сте when polite), “she is” is е.
- Not adding a pronoun every time: Добро сум already means “I'm well”.

</details>

**Grammar cards**

- 🃏 *Can сум start a sentence?* → No. Something comes first: Добро сум. (e.g. „Добро сум.“)

- ▢ Како ___ Марко? → **е** of [е / си / сум]: Marko is “he” → е.

### Casual ти or polite вие

**Agenda:** Choose ти for friends, вие for strangers and elders

**Rule card:** Macedonian has two words for “you”. Use ти with friends and family (Како си?) and вие with strangers, elders and groups (Како сте?). The verb matches: polite forms end in -те.

**Recap:** Today you chose between two “you”s. ти is the casual one: Здраво, Марко! Како си? вие is polite (and also “you all”): Добар ден! Како сте? The verb follows your choice, so polite forms end in -те: сте, Сакате ли кафе?, and the Можете ли да повторите? you've used since chapter 1. Notice the pairing in the greetings too: a casual Здраво goes with ти, a polite Добар ден with вие. With a stranger, start with вие.

<details><summary>Library page</summary>

Like many European languages, Macedonian splits “you” in two. ти is singular and familiar: friends, family, children, people your own age in relaxed settings. вие is for one person you address politely (a waiter, a shopkeeper, an older person, anyone you've just met in a formal setting) and also for any group. Everything that refers to “you” follows the choice: си or сте for “are”, and verb endings in -ш or -те (Сакаш or Сакате). The polite words you already know, Извинете and Повелете, are вие forms too.

**Why it's like this**
- Why does Можете ли да повторите? end in -те? It's the polite вие form, the natural choice when you ask a stranger to repeat.
- вие does double duty: polite “you” to one person, and “you all” to a group. Context tells you which.
- The greeting often signals the register: Здраво goes with ти, Добар ден with вие.
- Moving from вие to ти is a step toward friendliness. With older people, let them offer it.

**Common mistakes**
- Not mixing the two in one sentence: Како сте? (polite) or Како си? (casual), with the verb matching the “you” you picked.
- Not using ти with a waiter or shopkeeper you don't know: вие is the safe default.

### Three genders: еден, една, едно

**Agenda:** Match еден, една, едно to the noun's gender

**Rule card:** Every noun is masculine, feminine or neuter, and “a/one” matches it: еден сок, една вода, едно пиво. A rough guide: a consonant at the end means masculine, -а feminine, -о or -е neuter.

**Recap:** Today's big idea: every noun has a gender, and the words around it match. еден goes with masculine nouns (еден леб), една with feminine (една вода), едно with neuter (едно кафе). The ending usually gives it away: a consonant, -а, or -о/-е. “This” matches the same way: овој, оваа, ова. You met it in chapter 1 without noticing: Еден маж зборува брзо.

**Examples:**
- еден сок — *a juice (masculine)* `grammar:gender#0`
- една вода — *a water (feminine)* `grammar:gender#1`

- Да. Едно кафе и една вода — седумдесет денари. — *Yes. One coffee and one water — seventy denars.* `scenario:gen-s0-survive#4`

**Callbacks (you already say these):**

### Counting things

**Agenda:** Count things and ask the price

**Rule card:** After a number bigger than one, the noun goes plural: пет јаболка, десет денари. “Two” has two forms: два for masculine nouns, две for feminine and neuter. To ask a price: Колку чини?

**Recap:** Today you counted. “One” matches the noun (еден/една/едно); “two” is два or две; from three up, the number stays the same and the noun goes plural: пет јаболка, десет денари, седумдесет денари. To ask a price it's just Колку чини?, and you can point and add ова: Колку чини ова? Plurals get their own lesson in chapter 6; for now, learn јаболка and денари as the forms you hear after a number.

**Examples:**

- три пива — *three beers* `grammar:numbers#2`
- „Колку чини ова?“ — *"How much is this?"* `story:gen-s0-survive-story#6`

Numbers work in two steps. “One” agrees with the noun's gender, as in the last lesson: еден, една, едно. “Two” still shows gender: два with masculine nouns, две with feminine and neuter ones (две кафиња). From three up the number never changes, and the noun takes its plural form: три пива, пет јаболка, десет денари. To ask a price, use Колку чини? (“How much does it cost?”); add ова (“this”) when pointing: Колку чини ова? People are counted with special forms such as двајца (“two people”), which you only need to recognize for now.

**Why it's like this**
- Why јаболка (“apples”) after пет? From two up, the noun takes its plural form, just as пиво becomes пива in три пива. Plurals come in chapter 6; numbers are where you meet them first.
- Why два and две? “Two” is the one number after “one” that still matches gender: два for masculine, две for feminine and neuter.
- колку is both “how much” and “how many”: Колку чини? asks a price, Колку јаболка сака Ана? asks a count.
- Counting people has its own form, двајца (“two people”). Recognize it for now.

**Common mistakes**
- Not keeping the noun singular after a number: three beers is три пива, with the plural.
- Not using два with feminine or neuter nouns: две кафиња.
