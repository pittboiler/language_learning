# Familiar language, new stories (2026-10-14)

**Status:** approved by Jake on 2026-10-14 (decisions in §7). Step 1 is built on branch `writing-lines`. Next
steps are in §6.

> "It's the core LESSON that must be repeated, not the EXERCISE." Once a form is taught, every kind of practice
> uses all the forms taught so far: story, questions, Use it, Say it, Build, conversation, writing, warm-up.
> Each one puts them in a different context. That covers every person (I, you, he/she, we, you all, they), not
> just the 2–3 a single scene happens to need.

Jake's notes: it feels slow because of how much it repeats, not because of how much it teaches. It's the same
story ("Ана учи македонски") 7 times in chapter 1. The same questions and the same conversation come up session
after session. And we barely practise *you*, *you all*, *we* or *they*. He doesn't want more taught per chapter.
He wants what's already taught to turn up in new settings and new persons, and earlier chapters' settings to
come back later with new grammar.

**This proposal keeps every chapter's structure.** Same sessions, roles, points and words, so the course
`version` doesn't change and nobody gets moved back. What changes is what fills each session: which story,
which questions, which version of the conversation, and which lines you say.

## 2. The proposal

### 2a. Three stories per chapter, not one

Each chapter keeps its **home story** and gets two new ones:

- **A variation.** The same setting and words, but with other people. It's told so that other persons do the
  work: a dialogue between friends (ти), a polite exchange or a group (вие), or a "we"/"they" narrator.
- **A spiral story** (from chapter 3 on). It goes back to an earlier chapter's setting with this chapter's
  grammar. For example: chapter 9's past tense at chapter 4's café ("Вчера бевме во кафулето"), or chapter
  8's future at chapter 6's market ("Утре ќе одиме на пазар"). Familiar scene, new grammar. These replace most
  of today's word-for-word rereads.

There's also a **recurring cast**: Ана, Марко, Вера (an older neighbour, for вие), Ема and Петар, in Skopje
and Bitola. A spiral story then feels like a callback, not a stranger's story.

**Rules the planner follows** (and a lint checks):

- No story in two sessions in a row.
- At most two reads per chapter. Chapter 1 is the exception: its home story gets three reads, spread out, since
  there's nothing earlier to reread.
- A new story is at least 85% known words the first time it's read. Names and one or two words explained in the
  English line are fine.
- From chapter 4 on, every person shows up in at least two lines you read in the chapter, and at least once in
  something you say or write.

How the slots get filled:

| Chapter size | Home | Variation | Spiral | Reread of an earlier story (review day) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 story slots | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 6 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 5 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

That's **24 new stories** (2 per chapter), about 190 lines plus 3 questions each.

### 2b. Questions that ask about *you*, in other persons

Each new story's 3 questions include:
- one asked in a person other than "he/she" ("Каде сте вие?", "Што сакаат тие?"), and
- one that turns to the learner ("А ти? Каде си ти?").

I'd also add a new **"Switch the person"** exercise to "Use it", built from the verb tables (no new
Macedonian). A story line is shown with a new subject and the verb blanked, and you pick the right form:
*Ана пие кафе → Ние ___ кафе* (пиеме / пијам / пијат).

### 2c. A second version of each conversation

Each chapter gets one more version of its conversation. It's either the **other side** (you're the waiter, the
seller, the local giving directions) or a **group** (you order or plan for "we", or ask "you all"). Sessions
rotate: the first try is version A, the use days are B then A, and the checkpoint stays on A. Example, chapter 4
with you as the waiter:

> partner: Добар ден! · **you:** Добар ден! Што ќе сакате? · partner: Едно кафе и еден чај, ве молам. ·
> **you:** Сакате ли и вода? · partner: Не, фала. · **you:** Повелете. Кафето и чајот. · partner: Колку чини? ·
> **you:** Сто и педесет денари.

That's 12 new conversations, about 85 lines.

### 2d. "Say it" in other persons (no new Macedonian)

From chapter 4 on, one "Say it" line is today's build sentence in two persons, rotating by session (I + we,
you + they, you all + she…). Today the chapter-4 session might have *Сакам пиво*. With this it would have
*Сакам пиво* and *Сакаат пиво* ("They want beer"). The 75 build items already have all six persons.

### 2e. Teach the missing person forms, within capacity

These are words added to sessions that have room. That doesn't change the structure.
- **тие** in chapter 1, session 7 (which has only one new word).
- **им / ви** ("to them", "to you all") in chapter 8, sessions 6–7 (1 and 0 new words).
- **Past tense for we, you all and they** (бевме / беа, имавме / имаа, отидовме / отидоа) in chapter 9,
  sessions 5–6 (both 0 new words today). The past-tense point only teaches the I/you/he forms right now.

### 2f. Writing — already built, on branch `writing-lines`

Each chapter's writing session is now a scene plus 6–8 English lines to write in Macedonian, one at a time. Each
line uses only what's been taught by then (a lint checks this). The lines are deliberately spread across
persons. Chapter 4, for example, covers all six: *Сакаме две кафиња · Ана сака чај · Сакаш ли сок? · Имате ли
сок? · Сакаат две пива…* See §5.

## 5. Writing, line by line (built)

- A writing session now shows the scene. Then, one at a time: the English line, an optional hint, and a box
  (Latin letters are fine). **Check** accepts the model answer straight away, in Cyrillic or any usual Latin
  spelling (kje/ke/ḱe, ch/č/c, y/j…). Anything else goes to the tutor, which judges it against the model
  answer: other word orders and dropped pronouns count as right, but wrong person or formality doesn't. Then
  the model answer with 🔊, then the next line. The last screen shows every line: what you wrote next to the
  model. Misses go into the recap.
- Content: `packages/pack-mk/src/writing-lines.ts`, 12 chapters, 86 lines. Lint: `writing-untaught` (every
  word taught by that session; a taught adjective may take its agreeing endings once agreement is taught).
- Course `version` is unchanged (1vx4d6w). Only agenda wording changed.
